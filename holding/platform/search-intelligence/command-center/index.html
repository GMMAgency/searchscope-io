The headline

A change figure you can actually trust

Total Clicks, Impressions, Avg CTR and Avg Position, each with the change against the comparison period. The comparison is named in full underneath, with its dates, so there’s never a percentage without a period attached to it.

Compare against what you choose

Last month, last year, or one campaign

The previous period is the default: the same number of days immediately before. Year on year is the one to use when your demand has a shape to it, and a custom range is there for comparing against a specific campaign.

When the history isn’t there

It tells you rather than inventing a number

If your history starts inside the comparison window, the card reads no comparison data rather than inventing a percentage. A banner above the report explains which part of the window has no data behind it.

A zero it can’t stand behind

Doesn’t get shown as one

When the window you picked has no data behind it, the cards read still syncing rather than zero. That’s a new account waiting for its first import, and it’s also an established one filtered to a country it gets no traffic from.

The detail

Queries, pages, and the relationship between them

Four views over the same Search Console performance data, plus Impact Tracking. Move between them to understand what sits behind the headline numbers. Their search boxes query the whole dataset rather than the rows currently listed, so a term you can’t see is still findable.

Queries

The queries Search Console makes available

The highest-impression searches first, with branded ones badged, and the numbers sortable so you can work out what’s driving visibility and what’s driving traffic. They aren’t always the same searches.

Pages

See performance page by page

Open a page for its clicks and impressions over time, or send it to the Strategic Advisor with the performance context already attached.

Page + Queries

Which searches each page is winning

Use it when a page is moving and you want to see which queries moved with it. Every page expands into its own query table, fetched when you open it.

The finding, one click away

Analyzer issues, on the row

Where an analyzer has found something on a page, the row carries a chip that opens the relevant workspace already filtered to that URL.

Brand against non-brand

Separate existing brand demand from wider search discovery

Branded and non-branded searches tell you different things. Brand traffic is people who already have your name or your products in mind. Non-brand traffic is how often you’re being found through the wider topics and needs you compete for. The two cards here are how you tell them apart.

How it decides

A list of words you write

Any query containing one of your brand keywords counts as brand. It’s a plain text match, so put your company name, your product names and the misspellings people actually type into the list.

Set it on day one

Or the card reads zero

With nothing in that list, the brand card reads zero, and zero looks like a measurement. It isn’t. It means nothing has been told what your brand is. The button sits in the Command Center filter bar rather than in Settings.

Why the two don’t add up

And why that’s honest

The split reads a query-level table, and Google withholds the queries for a slice of traffic. So brand plus non-brand comes to less than the headline clicks. Adding the difference to one side would make the numbers tidy and wrong.

The baseline

The four weeks before you changed it

Searchscope captures it when you mark the change applied, and it stays fixed after that, so nothing quietly moves what you’re measuring against. Change the applied date and the baseline is recalculated, which the dialog says while you’re doing it.

Before and after

Clicks, impressions and position

Three cards per page, each showing the last 28 days, the change against the baseline, and what the baseline was. A page changed more than once keeps one card with a timeline on it, and the comparison stays against the first change.

Track changes Searchscope didn’t suggest

Add the page yourself

Marking a finding applied tracks the URL in the finding, which isn’t always the URL you changed. Fixed a cannibalization clash by rewriting the other page? Add that one by hand: the URL, what you changed, and the date.

It fills as you work

Nothing to set up

Nothing appears until you mark something applied or add a page yourself, so an empty tab on a new account is the expected state rather than a fault.

Filters

Set them once, save them, stop setting them

Date range, comparison, country, device and brand or non-brand, in one bar above the tabs. Any combination can be saved with a name, and one of them can be the default that loads with Command Center.

A saved filter is relative

So it doesn’t go stale

Save "non-brand, mobile, last 28 days" and it means the last 28 days every time you load it, not the 28 days that were current when you saved it.

The window ends where the data ends

Not at yesterday

Search Console typically runs a few days behind, so the ranges here end at the newest day actually imported. That’s why a 28-day window can end four days ago and still be 28 days.
