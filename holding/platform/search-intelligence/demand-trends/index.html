Demand is the market a month. Position is yours.

Topics, not keywords

Built from your own searches, then measured against the market

The analysis groups the queries in your Search Console into topics that mean roughly the same thing to a searcher. Then it asks a market data source what each one is actually searched for a month, per country.

Your side

The queries you already appear for

Grouped by meaning rather than by matching words, so "waterproof walking boots" and "boots for wet weather" are one topic rather than two.

The market’s side

What the topic is worth a month

Real search volumes, not impressions. Impressions tell you what you were shown for. Volume tells you how big the room is, including the part of it you’re not in.

Counted once

Close variants are folded together

Search tools report the same volume against every variant of a phrase. Left alone, a topic worth 1,900 searches reads as 5,700. Variants are folded into one before anything is totalled, and the ones that were folded are still listed underneath.

The chart

Demand up the side, your position across the bottom

Every bubble is a topic. Higher means more people are searching for it, further right means you rank worse for it, and the size of the bubble is the demand again so the big ones stay visible when they’re far right.

The shaded zone

High demand, weak position

Marked out because that combination is where the work usually is: anything at position 10 or worse with real volume behind it. It’s a position and volume zone and nothing else, so read the color of the bubble for the direction.

Color is the trend

Of the market, not of you

A falling topic in the shaded zone is a different decision from a rising one. The first is usually not worth chasing. The second is the reason this screen exists.

Click a bubble

And the list jumps to it

The chart and the list below it are the same set, filtered together. Clicking a bubble scrolls to that topic’s card rather than opening something new.

The figures above the list

Three numbers, and one of them is meant to be humbling

Clusters

Topics with real demand behind them

Only topics reaching 30 searches a month are counted, so a long tail of near-zero groups doesn’t pad the number.

Rising demand

How many are growing

Measured quarter against quarter, with a 10% threshold either way before a topic counts as rising or falling rather than flat.

Demand captured

The searches you turn into clicks

Usually a small percentage, and it should be: most searches never click any result at all. It’s a number to watch for direction rather than to judge in isolation.

The filter pills

Six ways to turn the list into a decision

Each one is a different question, and the number on it is how many topics match. A pill showing zero is a question you don’t have to ask today. Two more arrive with your first competitor, and they’re in the next section.

Losing share

The market is growing and you’re taking less of it

Demand rising while your impressions fall. Usually the one worth opening first, because it’s a change in your position rather than in the weather.

Rising + weak

Growing, and you’re off the top of page one

Demand rising with your average position worse than 8.

Rising + strong

High demand, no clicks

500 searches a month and not one click

You’re being shown and nobody is choosing you, so the title and description are worth reading before the ranking is.

Demand falling

The topic itself is shrinking

Whatever your position. Useful for deciding what to stop spending time on.

Cannibalized

More than one of your pages splitting a query

The Cannibalization workspace’s problem, seen from the topic end.

What arrives with a competitor

Two more filters, and a view of its own

Add a competitor in Competitive Landscape and two more pills switch on, plus a separate view. Until then they sit greyed rather than hidden, so you can see what you’re missing. All three compare against the competitors you named, using stored rankings rather than the live results, which is worth holding in mind before acting on any of them.

Competitor cluster gap

They’re ahead of you here

A competitor you track is in the top 20 and more than five places ahead, or ranks in the top 20 for keywords in the topic you have no presence for at all.

Uncontested

None of them is anywhere near it

Read it carefully: it’s about them, not you. Your own position isn’t part of the test, and the live results can still be owned by sites you don’t track.

Topics you don’t cover

A view, not a filter

Swaps the list for whole topics your competitors rank for where you have no page and no visibility, split into the ones nobody in your set wins and the ones a competitor already owns. With competitors in and nothing to show, it drops out of the row.

Where the volumes come from

Per country, cached, and set in one place

Volumes are pulled per market and stored, so opening the screen twice doesn’t pull them twice. Refresh re-pulls them when you want today’s figures.

One market at a time

Set in Settings

The market comes from Target market in Business Context, not from a picker on this screen. Change it there and the whole dataset changes, including the volumes, because they’re pulled and stored per country.

What a topic card carries

And what you can do with it

Twelve months of demand beside twelve months of your impressions, your strongest ranking page, and the keywords inside. Create Brief sends it to Content Studio, Ask Advisor puts it to your strategist, and Ignore moves it to an archive you can restore from.
